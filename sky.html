How it works: every star (5,000 real stars from the Hipparcos catalogue, down to the faintest the eye can see) has a fixed place on the sky sphere. From your latitude, longitude and the time, our code works out the sidereal time, and from it the star's height and direction. Times are in IST. "Visible tonight" means at least 10° above the horizon after dark. The star count uses the faintest star your sky allows (city glow, twilight, moonlight) and makes stars near the horizon look fainter, because their light passes through more air. Star data: d3-celestial (Hipparcos), IAU constellation lines.
